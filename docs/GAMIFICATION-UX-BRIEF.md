# Gamification: design workstream

Status: planned, awaiting UI/UX design. This does not block content implementation.
No gamification code or new reward metadata is part of the content expansion.

## Purpose

Help readers see their progress, return to Scripture, and choose a manageable next
step. Reward participation and learning without suggesting that app usage measures
faith, spiritual maturity, God's favour, or salvation.

## Daily flow to design

Open Today → choose or resume a reading → read and reflect → finish → receive
encouragement → see the next step. Include first use, a normal return, a missed
week, a completed plan, a changed goal, and optional participation.

## Screens and behaviour

- Today: reader-chosen goal, next reading, and gentle weekly rhythm.
- Completion: warm feedback and a clear option to continue or stop for the day.
- Personal journey: private reading history and meaningful completed milestones.
- Milestones: plan completion and reading journeys, without inflated achievement spam.
- Return: welcome after an absence, without a lost-progress penalty or guilt.
- Preferences: control encouragement, reminders, and visibility of rewards.

Design age-appropriate presentation for children, teens, and adults. Do not use
public leaderboards, faith scores, streak-loss threats, or compulsory competition.
Do not mark salvation as an achievement. Reflection answers should never be graded.

## Decisions to resolve before implementation

Define the visual language, goal choices, optionality, accessibility and reduced
motion, milestone rules, repeat-reading behaviour, and offline persistence. Decide
what counts as a reading without requiring private journal text or fabricated
proof of attention. Consider empty states and corrections after accidental taps.

Use the existing content and completion data where appropriate, but inspect its
semantics before using it for milestones. Do not introduce achievement requirements
into plan files until the design and tracking rules are settled.

## Review criteria

A reader should know what to read next, understand their progress, and feel able
to return after a break. Evaluate clarity, usefulness, and emotional pressure with
representative users; higher reading counts alone are not evidence of spiritual growth.
